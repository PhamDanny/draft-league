Luck score by player

Every random event in every replay is compared with its odds. A score of 0 is average luck, +1 is one standard deviation luckier than chance usually gives, and every lucky event for one player is an unlucky one for the other. Hover for the breakdown.

Where the luck came from

Each column is lucky events beyond the odds: positive means more good outcomes than the odds predicted, negative means fewer. Crits are also shown as actual against expected.

Every kind of event

Tries is how many times the event could have happened, happened is how many times it did, and expected is what the odds predicted. Luck is positive when the outcome favored that player.

Luck by game

The edge is how many lucky events beyond the odds the luckier player had in that game. The replay number opens the Showdown replay.

Most improbable moments

Non-crit events that went the way their odds said was unlikely, rarest first. Odds is the chance of exactly what happened.

How luck is measured

  • Luck score: for each random event, the outcome minus its odds. A 10% freeze that happens is +0.9 for the attacker and −0.9 for the target, and one that does not is −0.1 and +0.1. A player's net is the sum of those, and the score is the net divided by the spread chance alone would produce (the square root of the summed odds times one minus the odds). Zero is average and about two thirds of players should land between −1 and +1.
  • What is counted: critical hits (1 in 16, 1 in 8 for high-crit moves, 1 in 4 after Focus Energy), accuracy of every move under 100%, secondary burns, freezes, paralysis, flinches, confusion and stat drops, full paralysis (25%), thawing (20% a turn), and hitting yourself in confusion (50%).
  • Only real chances count: a secondary effect is only counted when the target could have been affected, so not if it fainted, was behind a Substitute, already had a status, was immune by type, or (for flinches) had already moved. Frost Breath always crits, so it is left out, as are Fake Out flinches.
  • Not counted: damage rolls, speed ties, sleep length, repeated Protect, and anything the replay does not show, such as Serene Grace, Shield Dust, Compound Eyes, Sand Veil, Scope Lens, Wide Lens and evasion or accuracy boosts. Odds use each move's Gen 5 base values.
  • Small samples: 33 games is not many random events, so a player a little above or below zero is within normal chance. Only the extremes mean much.

How the numbers are defined

  • Brought: the Pokémon appeared in battle at least once. A Pokémon that was picked but never sent out can't be seen in a replay, so it counts as not brought.
  • Bring % and lead %: out of the player's games. A lead is one of the two Pokémon on the field at the start.
  • KOs: credited to the last attack that damaged the Pokémon before it fainted. Burn, poison, sandstorm, Life Orb recoil, hazards and other indirect faints are not credited to anyone.
  • Sacrifices: Final Gambit, Explosion and similar moves where the user faints on its own.
  • Damage: percent of HP removed from opposing Pokémon by the Pokémon's own attacks, in full HP bars. It is capped at the target's remaining HP, so overkill is not counted. Hits on a partner (spread moves) are tracked separately as friendly fire in the data.
  • Player win % when brought: how often the player won the games where that Pokémon appeared. Small samples swing a lot.
  • Players and accounts: every Pokémon belongs to a player's team. Players who used more than one Showdown account have their accounts combined (see config/players.json).
  • Source: Pokémon Showdown replays of the Gen 5 Doubles Custom Game, parsed from each replay's battle log. Zoroark's Illusion can make its switch-ins show as another Pokémon.
